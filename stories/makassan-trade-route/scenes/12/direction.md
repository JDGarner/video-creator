# Scene 12 · A different way to understand Australian history

**Timecode:** 7:25--7:55  
**Purpose:** Resolve the story and leave the viewer with its wider significance.

## Visuals and animation

-   Return to the map of Australia and Indonesia.

-   Reintroduce the historic route.

-   Briefly montage trepang, praus, tamarind trees, archaeological
    remains and Yolŋu artwork.

-   End on an aerial or wide view of Arnhem Land's coastline.

-   Final title card:

    **AUSTRALIA'S HISTORY DIDN'T BEGIN IN 1788.**

-   End card: *The Voyage to Marege' --- Campbell Macknight, 1976.*

## Narration

The story of the Makassan trade is a reminder that Australia's history was never isolated from the rest of the world.

Long before British colonisation, northern Australia was connected to international commerce, cultural exchange and maritime travel.

Aboriginal communities were active participants in these relationships, trading, travelling and adapting new technologies while maintaining their own cultures and connections to Country.

The fleets may have disappeared, but their legacy remains in the landscape, in language, in art and in the memories of the people who live there.

Australia's international history didn't begin with the arrival of the British.

It was already underway, across the seas to the north.

## Production notes

-   Let the final images breathe. Avoid rushing the closing narration.
-   The closing title is a rhetorical summary, not a claim that all of
    Australia had the same external connections.
