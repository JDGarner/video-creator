# Scene 8 · Traces left behind

**Timecode:** 5:00--5:45  
**Purpose:** Reveal the enduring cultural and environmental evidence.

## Visuals and animation

Structure the scene as three evidence reveals.

**A. Tamarind trees** - Show a mature tamarind tree. - Locate a tree
near a former Makassan camp on a map, where a verified location is
available. - Label: *Introduced plant associated with Makassan visits*.

**B. Language** - Show a verified example of an Indonesian- or
Malay-derived word used in a northern Australian Aboriginal language. -
Display its meaning and the relevant language/community. - Avoid using a
word without checking its spelling, pronunciation, language and
etymology.

**C. Art and cultural memory** - Show Yolŋu bark paintings depicting
Makassan praus, with permission and correct attribution. - Highlight the
vessel in the artwork. - Compare it with a historically grounded vessel
illustration.

## Narration

Although the Makassans eventually stopped coming, evidence of their visits remained.

One visible trace is the tamarind tree. Tamarind trees found near some former Makassan camps are associated with the visiting crews and may reflect the introduction or cultivation of the plant.

Then there is language.

Words of Indonesian and Malay origin entered some northern Australian Aboriginal languages, reflecting generations of contact.

And perhaps most remarkably, the relationship became part of Aboriginal art and cultural memory.

Yolŋu artists have depicted Makassan sailing vessels in bark paintings, preserving memories of the ships and the people who visited their Country.

These are not just remnants of an old trade. They are evidence of a relationship that became embedded in the cultural history of northern Australia.

## Production notes

-   A tamarind tree's presence alone cannot prove when it was planted or
    who planted it. Use "associated with" rather than asserting a
    definite planting event unless a particular tree has documented
    provenance.
-   The earlier draft used "rrupiya" as an example for money. Verify the
    exact form, language and etymology with a linguistic source before
    including it. If uncertain, omit the word rather than risk a
    misleading example.
-   Obtain permission and attribution for Aboriginal artworks. Do not
    treat culturally significant art as generic stock imagery.
