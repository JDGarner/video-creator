# Short: The Makassan Trade Route

**Deliverable:** Voice-over-led map explainer, YouTube Short
**Target runtime:** 55–60 seconds
**Format:** One continuous animated map with overlays. No talking head, no diagram slides.
**Source:** cut down from `stories/longform/makassan-trade-route/makassan_trade_route_video_storyboard.md`. All editorial safeguards in that document still apply (verify 1803, 1907, the Flinders encounter, the "1,500 km" figure, and qualify tamarind and language claims).

## The idea in one line

Centuries before the British, fleets from Indonesia sailed to northern Australia every year, and the two worlds traded, mixed and left traces that are still there.

## Narration

1. Long before the British arrived, northern Australia was already part of an international trade network.
2. Every year, fleets from Makassar, in today's Indonesia, sailed over a thousand kilometres south across the Arafura Sea.
3. They came for sea cucumber. Dried, it was a delicacy in China, and worth the voyage.
4. They rode the monsoon down in December, and the same winds home months later.
5. On the Arnhem Land coast they built camps, boiling and drying the catch for the journey back.
6. But this coast wasn't empty. Aboriginal communities had lived here for tens of thousands of years, and they traded too: for metal, cloth and tools. Some sailed back to Makassar.
7. The traces remain. Indonesian words in Aboriginal languages. Tamarind trees at old camps. Bark paintings of the ships.
8. In 1803, Matthew Flinders met six of these vessels. The last recorded voyage was 1907.
9. Australia's history didn't begin in 1788. It was already underway, across the sea to the north.

## Production notes

- Vertical 9:16 is the Shorts format. The map camera needs a portrait fit: the Sulawesi to Arnhem Land route runs north-west to south-east, so a portrait frame can hold it at a slightly tighter zoom than the landscape version.
- Keep it to one accent colour. The route, the wind arrows and the date markers share it. Nothing else does.
- Beat 3's trepang image is the only photographic element. If no licensed image is available, use a line-drawn glyph.
- Beat 6's glyphs for goods are schematic. Do not invent a dramatic encounter.
- Beat 7's language example needs a verified word before it can be shown. Until then the pin reads *loanwords*.
- Captions are rendered in the composition from the narration alignment (see `voice/PLAN.md` step 7), two to four words at a time, centred at about 72 percent of frame height. Keep that band clear of other overlays.
