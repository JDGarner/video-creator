# Scene 4 · The journey to Marege'

**Timecode:** 2:00--2:45  
**Purpose:** Explain the geography, destination and seasonal sailing pattern.

## Visuals and animation

This is a key animated-map sequence.

Map labels: - Makassar, Sulawesi - Timor - Arafura Sea - Arnhem Land -
Northern Australia

Animation: - A fleet of several praus leaves Makassar. - A dotted route
line travels south towards Arnhem Land. - Add restrained wave movement
and wind arrows to suggest monsoon patterns. - Place the label
**MAREGE'** over the relevant northern Australian coast. - Show the
return journey as a second route or a differently styled line.

## Narration

The destination was a region the Makassans called Marege', referring to the northern Australian coast, particularly the Arnhem Land region.

The voyages were timed around the monsoon seasons.

Typically, fleets departed Makassar around December, travelling south across the Indonesian archipelago and the Arafura Sea.

They would arrive during the northern Australian wet season and spend several months harvesting trepang.

This seasonal rhythm was crucial.

The same broad pattern of seasonal winds helped make the return voyage possible.

And year after year, the fleets came back.

## Production notes

-   Treat the route as a schematic reconstruction, not a precise GPS
    track for every voyage.
-   The timing varied. Avoid presenting December and March--April as
    fixed dates for every fleet.
-   "Marege'" is a Makassan term for the northern Australian region; do
    not imply it always referred to exactly the same modern
    administrative boundary.
