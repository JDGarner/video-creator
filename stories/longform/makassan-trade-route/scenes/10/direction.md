## Narration

By the beginning of the nineteenth century, the trade was well established.

In 1803, British explorer Matthew Flinders encountered a fleet of six Makassan vessels off the northern Australian coast.

Through an interpreter, he spoke with their leader, a man named Pobassoo.

The encounter became one of the important European records of the industry.

It illustrates something remarkable: while European explorers were still documenting parts of Australia's northern coastline, Indonesian traders were already familiar with it through generations of commercial voyages.
