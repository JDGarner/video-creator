# Scene 2 · The commodity connecting three worlds

**Timecode:** 0:40--1:20  
**Purpose:** Explain what trepang is and why it motivated the voyages.

## Visuals and animation

-   Underwater illustration or footage of sea cucumbers on a tropical
    seabed.

-   Show a sea cucumber being collected.

-   Transition to dried trepang.

-   Introduce a simple three-part trade graphic:

    **Makassar / Sulawesi → Northern Australia → Chinese markets**

-   Animate a line connecting the three regions.

## Narration

The commodity was trepang, also known as bêche-de-mer or sea cucumber.

Although it might look unremarkable, dried sea cucumber was highly prized in Chinese cuisine.

Demand from Chinese markets created an opportunity for Indonesian maritime traders.

And one of the harvesting grounds was thousands of kilometres to the south, along the coast of northern Australia.

The trade connected Indonesian fishing communities, Aboriginal Australians and Chinese consumers in a commercial network spanning the region.

## Production notes

-   Use the terms *trepang* and *bêche-de-mer* clearly; explain that
    both refer to processed sea cucumber.
-   Do not imply all trepang was consumed in China directly from
    Australia. It was carried through Makassar and wider trading
    networks.
