# Reference recording script

Read in the voice of the channel: calm, documentary, unhurried. About 60 seconds. Record 3 or 4 takes.

---

- In 1606, Captain Willem Janszoon arrived at ... <to the western coast of the Cape York Peninsula near modern-day Weipa>
- His crew had the first recorded landfall and conflict with local Aboriginal people (such as the Wik and Yolngu communities)
- But it wasn't the first recorded contact and interaction with the Aboriginal people.
- 

Long before the first European ships reached the north coast of Australia, fleets from Sulawesi were already crossing the Arafura Sea every year.

They came for trepang. Dried sea cucumber. A delicacy worth more, by weight, than almost anything else a small wooden boat could carry.

So how far is it, really? From Makassar to Arnhem Land is about fifteen hundred kilometres. In a good season, with the monsoon behind them, the crossing took a couple of weeks.

By 1803, when Matthew Flinders met six of their vessels off the coast, the trade had been running for generations. The last recorded voyage was in 1907.

That's the story we're going to tell. Not as a footnote to someone else's history, but as a history in its own right.
